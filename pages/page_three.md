# Useful Open Source Software for Literary Studies

## This page explores Voyant Tools, an open-source project that could be useful for studying literature

As a major in Nordic literature, one might use the open-source program Voyant Tools for analysis of larger singular works or corpuses. Voyant Tools was made by Stéfan Sinclair and led by him until August 2020. From then on until today it’s led by Geoffrey Rockwell from the University of Alberta, with support by Andrew MacDonald and Cecily Raynor from McGill University. 

Voyant Tools is helpful to “distant read”, as opposed to close reading. This might show structures previously unknown, such as how often a word is used in a specific category of text or how specific authors focus on words in context, which would be helpful if your major examines literary geography. In this thematic perspective of literature, you could for example examine “geographic attention”, which is when you examine how much attention an author or a specific category of text has on specific places. This is explicitly done in “Hamsuns litterære geografi” (2020) by Boasson and Johnsen, which examines how much attention the Norwegian author Knut Hamsun has on different places. 

Voyant Tools uses a GPL3 license. The benefits with a GPL3 license are that it lets its users free use and modification. However, if you were to modify it and distribute your modification, you have to make the source available under the same license. This is to make sure the modifications also remain open source. 

The last commit was done three weeks ago, and there’s a total of nine contributors. There is an active issues-reporting system on their GitHub, but some of the issues date as far back as 2021. At the same time, there are newer issues dating to last week, so it’s difficult to say whether they are answered or not. 

Voyant Tools is supported by universities, through research grants. 

This tool could add some efficacy when it comes to my major. In any text-analysis it could help to see structures before hidden and provide alternative angles of interpretations. At the same time, I would not depend on it, it would simply make my life easier.
