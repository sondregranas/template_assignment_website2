# Open Source Software in Nordic Literary Studies

## This page explores an open-source project that could be useful for studying literature.

As a major in Nordic literature, one might use the open-source program Voyant Tools for analysis of larger singular works or corpuses. Voyant Tools is helpful to “distant read”, which means to look at texts or corpuses at a distant, as opposed to close reading them. This might show structures previously unknown, such as how often a word is used in a specific category of text or how specific authors focus on words in context, which would be helpful if your major examines literary geography. In this thematic perspective of literature, you could for example examine “geographic attention”, which is when you examine how much attention an author or a specific category of text has on specific places. This is explicitly done in Hamsuns litterære geografi (2020) by Boasson and Johnsen, who examines how much attention the Norwegian author Knut Hamsun has on different places. 

Voyant Tools was made by Stéfan Sinclair and led by him until August 2020. From then on until today it’s led by Geoffrey Rockwell from the University of Alberta, with support by Andrew MacDonald and Cecily Raynor from McGill University. 

Voyant Tools uses a GPL3 license, as well as a Creative Commons By Attribution license. The benefits with a GPL3 license are that it lets its users free use and modification. However, if you were to modify it and distribute your modification, you have to make the source available under the same license. This is to make sure the modifications also remain open source. 

The last commit was done three weeks ago, on 20th October and there’s a total of nine contributors. There is an active issues-reporting system on their GitHub, but some of the issues brought forth date as far back as 2021. At the same time, there are newer issues dating to last week, so it’s difficult to say whether they are answered or not. 

Lastly, Voyant Tools is partly economically supported by universities, through research grants and of course depend on volunteer contributions. 

This tool definitely adds some efficacy when it comes to my major. If I am to work with literary geography or generally larger text analysis, it could help with seeing structures before hidden and provide different angles of interpretation. At the same time, I would not depend on it, it would simply make my life easier, so I could work without it as well.
