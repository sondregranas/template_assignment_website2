# Disadvantages of Open Source Software

## Maintenance, accessibility and security

Open-source software also presents some challenges considering uncertainty around maintenance, accessibility, and security. 

Open source is heavily dependent on volunteer work, so one disadvantage, and possible complication is if contributors lose interest. This would over time lead to programs being outdated and that would be problematic for institutions who depend on them. 

This also leads on to another disadvantage of open source, namely the technological knowledge that is required to keep updating it. The mentioned problem with volunteers seizing to contribute would not be a problem if it weren’t for this. One of the biggest advantages of open source is as mentioned that anybody might access it, but without the knowledge of how to interact with the technology, the advantage turns to a disadvantage. 

Security is also a factor separating open source from closed source software, but whether it’s a disadvantage for open source is nuanced. Having the software open for everyone obviously comes with a risk of someone misusing their possibility for contributions. On the other hand, with more people looking at the code, more people could identify security breaches. This is exactly what happened when Andres Freund discovered an attack on a compression tool used in Linux systems. 

So, there’s obviously nuances to the pros and cons of open source, as showed on this page and the previous. It should not automatically be considered better than closed source, since there’s advantages and disadvantages of them both. Open source is suitable in some instances. But its suitability depends on volunteers, that the programs are technologically accessible and that the security risk they might present is taken seriously.
