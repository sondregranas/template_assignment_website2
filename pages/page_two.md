# Disadvantages of Open Source Software

## Maintenance, accessibility and security

Even though open-source software offers the advantages mentioned on the previous page, and more, it also presents some challenges considering uncertainty regarding maintenance, accessibility, and security. 

Open source is heavily dependent on volunteer work, so one disadvantage, and possible complication is if contributors lose interest. This would over time lead to programs being outdated and that would be problematic for institutions who depend on the programs. 

This also leads to another disadvantage of open source, namely the technological knowledge that is required by those volunteers to keep updating it. The mentioned problem with volunteers seizing to contribute would not be a problem if it weren’t for this. One of the biggest advantages of open source is as mentioned that anybody might access it, but without the knowledge of how to interact with the technology, can you really access it?

Security is also a factor separating open source from closed source software. In a closed source, only a few people might access the software, making the security depend on whether we trust those people or not. Open source, on the other hand, is open for everyone, and there’s no guarantee that anybody is actively looking after it. Even though, this cannot be seen as a complete disadvantage of open source. It more nuanced than that. With a closed source, and, let’s say, a handful of people looking over the software, there might be easy to overlook some security threats, whereas open source, with an unlimited number of contributors might stand stronger. This is exactly what happened when Andres Freund discovered an attack on a compression tool used in Linux systems. 

So, there’s obviously nuances to the pros and cons of open source. As showed on this page, it should not automatically be considered better than closed source, just because of the pros mentioned on the previous. Open source is suitable in some instances, and it has some clear advantages. But its suitability depends on volunteers, that the programs are technologically accessible and that the security risk they might present is taken seriously.
